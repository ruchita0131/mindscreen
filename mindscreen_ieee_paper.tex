\begin{abstract}
Early identification of depressive symptoms and explicit high-risk indicators
can support timely access to mental-health resources.
This paper presents \textbf{MindScreen}, a tri-modal prototype that integrates
Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) responses, transformer-based text
emotion classification, and an audio-payload-based production heuristic
through decision-level late fusion.
A rule-based \textbf{High-Risk Escalation (HRE)} module is introduced to
prevent predefined indicators---including positive responses to PHQ-9
Item~9, high questionnaire scores, and selected crisis-related
keywords---from being suppressed by modality averaging.
The system also integrates \textbf{Saathi}, an LLM-assisted conversational
companion with multi-turn context and crisis-resource routing to national
helpline information.
Evaluation focuses on deterministic rule execution under three manually
constructed masking scenarios and operational latency and memory profiling
under a single-request cloud-container configuration.
The results demonstrate the specified behavior of the escalation rules and
the measured performance of the prototype, but do not establish clinical
validity, diagnostic accuracy, or real-world safety.
The acoustic production branch uses a payload-size heuristic and should not
be interpreted as a validated acoustic biomarker model.
Future work will address labeled clinical evaluation, probability calibration,
robust acoustic feature extraction, and systematic assessment of
conversational safety.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) affects over 280 million individuals globally
and stands as a leading contributor to non-fatal health
impairment~\cite{who2023}.
In lower- and middle-income nations such as India, the mental healthcare
treatment gap exceeds 75\%--85\%, driven by severe shortages of licensed
psychiatrists (approximately 0.75 per 100{,}000 population), geographic
concentration of healthcare facilities in urban centers, and widespread
socio-cultural stigma~\cite{gururaj2016}.

% ── Minor Comment 2: "global clinical benchmark" → precise wording ────────
The PHQ-9~\cite{kroenke2001} is a widely used depression symptom-severity
screening instrument.
However, static psychometric self-reports suffer from notable operational
limitations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Subjective Recall Bias:} Patients frequently alter responses
    to conform to perceived social norms or reflect transient acute emotional
    states during survey completion.
  \item \textbf{Contextual Insensitivity:} Discrete Likert-scale questions
    cannot capture qualitative life stressors, interpersonal dynamics, or
    cognitive distortion patterns.
  \item \textbf{Omission of Physiological Biomarkers:} Self-reports cannot
    measure somatic or psychomotor correlates of depression, such as speech
    monotony and vocal acoustic flattening~\cite{cummins2015,yang2023}.
\end{itemize}

Recent developments in affective computing have utilized transformer language
models~\cite{ji2022,xu2024} and vocal acoustic
analysis~\cite{scherer2013,cummins2015} to identify behavioral markers of
depression.
However, integrating heterogeneous signals into a clinical decision-support
pipeline presents significant algorithmic challenges.
Early-stage feature concatenation is highly sensitive to modality missingness
and obscures the explainability of individual channels.
More critically, unconstrained probabilistic averaging introduces a potential
\textit{false-negative dilution}: if a patient expresses acute suicidal intent
in text or on the PHQ-9 but exhibits calm vocal acoustics, linear averaging
can dilute the composite risk below the high-risk threshold.

% ── P0: Contributions reframed to match evaluation evidence ───────────────
To address these challenges, this paper presents \textbf{MindScreen},
a tri-modal prototype for depression screening support.
The primary contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Tri-Modal Prototype Architecture:}
    A decision-level late-fusion pipeline integrating PHQ-9 scoring,
    text-based affective classification, and an audio-payload-based
    production heuristic, preserving modality independence under missing
    inputs.
  \item \textbf{Rule-Based High-Risk Escalation (HRE):}
    A deterministic decision layer that escalates predefined questionnaire and
    text indicators (PHQ-9 Item~9 $> 0$, $S \ge 20$, or crisis text tokens)
    independently of the fused score, preventing their suppression by modality
    averaging.
  \item \textbf{Rule Execution Verification:}
    Evaluation of the escalation logic under three manually constructed masking
    scenarios designed to test selected high-risk conditions
    (Table~\ref{tab:safety}).
  \item \textbf{Conversational Resource-Routing Component:}
    Integration of an LLM-assisted conversational companion with rule-based
    crisis-resource detection and display of national helpline information
    (Tele-MANAS, KIRAN).
  \item \textbf{Prototype Performance Profiling:}
    Measurement of selected local and cloud-augmented pipeline components
    under a specified single-request benchmark configuration
    (Table~\ref{tab:latency}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and State-of-the-Art Taxonomy}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Psychometric Screening Instruments}
% ── Minor Comment 2: Precise PHQ-9 description ───────────────────────────
The PHQ-9 is a 9-item validated self-report instrument with an established
literature base for depression symptom-severity
screening~\cite{kroenke2001}.
Items are scored 0 to 3, yielding an aggregate score $S \in [0, 27]$.
Standard clinical guidelines define five severity intervals: minimal (0--4),
mild (5--9), moderate (10--14), moderately severe (15--19), and severe
(20--27)~\cite{kroenke2001}.
In digital screening workflows, categories are frequently collapsed into a
4-tier decision schema where scores $S \ge 15$ indicate elevated clinical
concern~\cite{kessler2003}.
However, standalone questionnaire systems lack behavioral verification against
somatic or vocal markers.

\subsection{Natural Language Processing in Affective Psychiatry}
Transformer architectures have achieved high benchmarks in computational
psychiatry.
Ji~et~al.~\cite{ji2022} developed MentalBERT and MentalRoBERTa,
demonstrating that domain-specific pre-training on mental health corpora
outperforms general language models on depressive symptom identification.
Xu~et~al.~\cite{xu2024} established that instruction-tuned large language
models can extract cognitive distortions from patient narratives.
Distilled RoBERTa models fine-tuned on emotion
categorization~\cite{hartmann2022} provide discriminative representations
across valence and arousal dimensions.
In production settings, pairing hosted neural transformers with deterministic
lexical fallbacks can support service continuity when remote inference is
unavailable.

\subsection{Acoustic Speech Biomarkers}
Depression induces observable neuro-motor changes in speech production,
including reduced vocal fold tension, restricted articulatory velocity, and
flattened prosody~\cite{cummins2015,scherer2013}.
Studies on the Distress Analysis Interview Corpus
(DAIC-WOZ)~\cite{gratch2014} have indicated that $F_0$ variation, MFCCs,
and vocal intensity may serve as indicators of distress.
Comprehensive acoustic extraction requires significant memory resources;
cloud-deployed systems therefore face practical constraints in applying full
spectrogram-based analysis.

\section{System Methodology and Mathematical Formulation}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Observation Space}
Let the input space for an assessment session be defined as a tuple
$\mathcal{X} = (\mathbf{q},\, T,\, \mathbf{a})$, where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathbf{q} = [q_1, \dots, q_9] \in \{0,1,2,3\}^9$ denotes the
    discrete PHQ-9 survey responses;
  \item $T \in \mathcal{V}^*$ represents the free-form text journal entry; and
  \item $\mathbf{a} \in \mathcal{A}$ represents the digitized speech audio
    signal.
\end{itemize}

% ── P1 / Major Comment 4: Four-class taxonomy explicitly defined ──────────
\subsection{Four-Category Screening Taxonomy}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

The system maps PHQ-9 scores to a system-specific 4-category screening
taxonomy (Table~\ref{tab:phq_mapping}).
This taxonomy is a system-design decision, not the standard 5-tier PHQ-9
clinical interpretation.
The two highest PHQ-9 clinical categories (moderately severe: 15--19; severe:
20--27) are combined into a single high-severity screening category.
This design choice reflects the system's use case as a preliminary screening
tool: both score ranges indicate a need for professional follow-up, and the
implementation treats both as requiring immediate priority-score assignment
via the HRE module (Section~\ref{sec:hre}).
Researchers using MindScreen output should not interpret the ``severe''
screening category as equivalent to the standard PHQ-9 clinical severity
label ``severe (20--27)''.

\begin{table}[!t]
\caption{System-Defined Four-Category Screening Taxonomy Derived from PHQ-9}
\label{tab:phq_mapping}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{PHQ-9 Score} & \textbf{Standard PHQ-9 Category}
  & \textbf{MindScreen Category} & \textbf{Design Rationale} \\
\midrule

5--9  & Mild              & Mild     & Direct mapping; monitoring recommended \\

We define the system's 4-category output space as:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C} = \{c_0\!:\!\text{minimal},\;
                  c_1\!:\!\text{mild},\;
                  c_2\!:\!\text{moderate},\;
                  c_3\!:\!\text{severe}\}.
  \label{eq:label_space}
\end{equation}
The objective is to map $\mathcal{X}$ to a normalized heuristic score
distribution $\mathbf{p}^F \in \Delta^3$, from which the final screening
category $\hat{y} \in \mathcal{C}$ and a priority score $c \in [0,1]$ are
derived.
% ── P0: "calibrated probability distribution" replaced throughout ─────────
Note that $\mathbf{p}^F$ is a normalized score vector constructed from
heuristic mappings; it has not been demonstrated to be a calibrated
probability distribution in the statistical sense (i.e., predicted values
do not have empirically verified correspondence with observed class
frequencies).

\subsection{Individual Modality Processing Pipelines}

\subsubsection{Questionnaire Branch ($\mathbf{p}^Q$)}
The clinical score $S$ is calculated by summing the 9 ordinal responses:
\begin{equation}
  S = \sum_{i=1}^{9} q_i, \quad S \in [0, 27].
  \label{eq:phq_score}
\end{equation}
A piecewise mapping function $\Phi_Q: [0, 27] \to \Delta^3$ aligns clinical
cutoff intervals with the 4-category space using fixed probability vectors:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}^Q =
  \begin{cases}
    [0.80,\;0.15,\;0.05,\;0.00]^T, & 0  \le S \le 4  \\
    [0.10,\;0.70,\;0.15,\;0.05]^T, & 5  \le S \le 9  \\
    [0.00,\;0.15,\;0.70,\;0.15]^T, & 10 \le S \le 14 \\
    [0.00,\;0.05,\;0.15,\;0.80]^T, & 15 \le S \le 27
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:phq_probs}
\end{equation}
These vectors are system-defined constants, not estimated from labeled data.
Scores $S \ge 15$ map to the high-severity tier as defined in
Table~\ref{tab:phq_mapping}.

\subsubsection{NLP Text Branch ($\mathbf{p}^T$)}
% ── Major Comment 8: Emotion mapping framed as heuristic ─────────────────
Free-text reflections $T$ are processed by an emotion classification
transformer (DistilRoBERTa~\cite{hartmann2022}), accessed via the HuggingFace
Inference API.
The model outputs an emotion class $e^* \in \mathcal{E}$ with confidence
$s \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
  (e^*,\, s) =
    \arg\max_{e \in \mathcal{E}}\,
    P\!\left(e \mid T;\, \Theta_{\text{NLP}}\right).
  \label{eq:nlp_argmax}
\end{equation}
A heuristic affective-state-to-risk mapping
$\Gamma: \mathcal{E} \to \{0,1,2,3\}$ translates general emotion labels into
screening categories.
This mapping is a design approximation; general emotion classification is
not equivalent to depression severity assessment, and the relationship between
these emotion categories and clinical depression severity has not been
empirically validated in this work:
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma(e^*) =
  \begin{cases}
    0\;(\text{minimal}), & e^* \in \{\text{joy},\;\text{neutral},\;\text{surprise}\} \\
    1\;(\text{mild}),    & e^* \in \{\text{fear},\;\text{anger}\} \\
    2\;(\text{moderate}),& e^* \in \{\text{sadness},\;\text{disgust}\}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:emotion_map}
\end{equation}
Let $r = \Gamma(e^*)$.
Lexical scanning checks $T$ for a defined crisis token set
$\mathcal{K}_{\text{crisis}} = \{\text{``suicide''},\;\text{``kill''},\;
\text{``hopeless''},\;\text{``worthless''}\}$.
If $\exists\,w \in T \cap \mathcal{K}_{\text{crisis}}$, then $r \leftarrow 3$.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations of the crisis token set.}
Simple keyword matching does not handle negation (``I do not want to kill
myself''), sarcasm, third-party references (``my friend said she wanted to
die''), indirect expressions (``I watched a film about suicide''), or
colloquial usage (``I feel worthless because I failed an exam'').
The token list is a minimal heuristic prototype;
systematic false-positive and false-negative behavior has not been evaluated.

The non-normalized score vector $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^4$ is:
\begin{equation}
  b_k =
  \begin{cases}
    s,    & k = r \\
    0.10, & k \neq r
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:score_vector}
\end{equation}
and the normalized heuristic score distribution $\mathbf{p}^T$ is:
\begin{equation}
  p_k^T = \frac{b_k}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{3} b_j},
  \quad \forall k \in \{0,1,2,3\}.
  \label{eq:nlp_normalize}
\end{equation}
When remote inference is unreachable, an internal deterministic keyword rule
engine selects fallback distributions to support service continuity when the
API is unavailable.

\subsubsection{Acoustic Processing Branch ($\mathbf{p}^A$)}
\label{sec:acoustic}
MindScreen implements a two-tier acoustic architecture.

\noindent\textbf{Offline Research Model.}
A 3-layer PyTorch MLP ($30 \!\to\! 128 \!\to\! 64 \!\to\! 4$) with Batch
Normalization, ReLU, and Dropout ($p = 0.3$) was designed around a
30-dimensional acoustic feature vector aligned with the
DAIC-WOZ corpus~\cite{gratch2014}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_A =
    \bigl[F_{0,\mu},\; F_{0,\sigma},\; \text{ZCR},\; \text{RMS},\;
          \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{MFCC}_{1:13}},\;
          \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{MFCC}_{1:13}}\bigr]^T
    \in \mathbb{R}^{30}.
  \label{eq:acoustic_features}
\end{equation}
The dimensional breakdown is: 2 ($F_0$ statistics) + 1 (ZCR) + 1 (RMS) + 13
(MFCC means) + 13 (MFCC standard deviations) = 30.
Features are intended to be computed per utterance (mean/std aggregation over
frames); target window size is 25~ms with 10~ms hop at 16~kHz.
MFCC coefficients~1--13 are used (excluding the zeroth energy coefficient).
$F_0$ handling for unvoiced frames and feature normalization procedures are
implementation-level details; training, validation, and test results for this
checkpoint have not been reported in this paper.
The model checkpoint is included in the repository for reproducibility of the
architecture; it should \textbf{not} be used as evidence of acoustic-biomarker
effectiveness.

\noindent\textbf{Edge Production Proxy.}
% ── P0 Major Comment 2: Proxy clearly labeled as engineering heuristic ────
Under cloud-container memory limits ($<$512~MB RAM), the system evaluates
audio using an audio-payload-size heuristic.
This heuristic is not a validated acoustic biomarker estimator.
Decoded payload size is affected by recording duration, encoder settings,
codec, bitrate, browser behavior, and audio content; it is not equivalent to
measured acoustic energy or validated vocal biomarkers.
The threshold values (5~kB and 30~kB) and the corresponding fixed probability
vectors are engineering design choices, not estimates derived from labeled
acoustic data.
The heuristic is retained to enable end-to-end architecture testing under
resource constraints.
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}^A =
  \begin{cases}
    [0.15,\;0.20,\;0.55,\;0.10]^T, & B < 5\;\text{kB}
      \;(\text{Short payload}) \\
    [0.20,\;0.55,\;0.20,\;0.05]^T, & 5 \le B < 30\;\text{kB}
      \;(\text{Medium payload}) \\
    [0.65,\;0.20,\;0.10,\;0.05]^T, & B \ge 30\;\text{kB}
      \;(\text{Long payload}) \\
    [0.55,\;0.25,\;0.15,\;0.05]^T, & \text{Recording omitted}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:acoustic_proxy}
\end{equation}
where $B = |\text{Base64Decode}(\mathbf{a})|\,/\,1024$ (kB).

\noindent\textbf{Note on reported results scope.}
All tri-modal fusion evaluations (Section~\ref{sec:eval}), HRE verification
scenarios (Table~\ref{tab:safety}), and latency profiling
(Table~\ref{tab:latency}) use the \emph{Edge Production Proxy}
\eqref{eq:acoustic_proxy}.
The offline PyTorch MLP is a research artifact included for reproducibility;
it is not active in the deployed cloud instance and is not used to generate
any result reported in this paper.

\subsection{Decision-Level Late Fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

The late-fusion module computes a convex combination of the three modality
score vectors:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{u} = w_T\,\mathbf{p}^T + w_A\,\mathbf{p}^A + w_Q\,\mathbf{p}^Q,
  \label{eq:fusion_weighted}
\end{equation}
% ── Major Comment 7: "empirically selected" corrected ─────────────────────
where manually specified weights based on the authors' design assumptions
satisfy $\sum_m w_m = 1.0$, with $w_T = 0.50$, $w_A = 0.30$, and
$w_Q = 0.20$.
These weights reflect assumed relative informativeness of each modality:
text is given the highest weight as the primary source of subjective
expression, followed by audio-based speech activity, with PHQ-9 providing a
psychometrically anchored baseline.
The weights have not been empirically optimized; optimization via
cross-validation on labeled clinical data is identified as future work.

% ── Moderate Comment 6: Normalization redundancy acknowledged ─────────────
Since each $\mathbf{p}^m$ lies on the probability simplex
($\sum_k p_k^m = 1$) and $\sum_m w_m = 1$, the weighted sum $\mathbf{u}$
already satisfies $\sum_k u_k = 1$.
Normalization is therefore redundant under these assumptions but is retained
as a defensive implementation step to handle floating-point accumulation and
any future change in input guarantees:
\begin{equation}
  p_k^F = \frac{u_k}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{3} u_j},
  \quad \forall k \in \{0,1,2,3\}.
  \label{eq:fusion_normalize}
\end{equation}
The base screening category $\hat{y}$ and priority score $c$ are:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = \arg\max_{k \in \mathcal{C}}\,p_k^F,
  \qquad
  c = \max_{k \in \mathcal{C}}\,p_k^F.
  \label{eq:prediction}
\end{equation}

% ── P1 Major Comment 6: CSO renamed HRE, decision table added ─────────────
\subsection{Rule-Based High-Risk Escalation (HRE) Module}
\label{sec:hre}

In a screening support tool, statistical averaging must not suppress
explicitly defined high-risk indicators.
We formalize two deterministic safety bounds.
The HRE module implements rule execution; it does not validate clinical safety
or crisis detection sensitivity.

\noindent\textbf{(i) High-Score Override.}
If $S \ge 20$ (Kroenke~et~al.~\cite{kroenke2001}), the screening category
is deterministically set to severe.
The priority score is set to a rule-based floor value, not a calibrated
posterior probability:
\begin{equation}
  S \ge 20 \;\Rightarrow\;
    \hat{y} \leftarrow \text{severe},\quad
    c \leftarrow \max(0.90,\; c).
  \label{eq:hre_severe}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{(ii) Explicit Indicator Flag.}
The crisis flag $C_F$ is raised when any of the following explicit conditions
holds:
\begin{equation}
  C_F = (S \ge 20) \;\lor\; (q_9 > 0)
        \;\lor\; (\hat{y} = c_3)
        \;\lor\; \text{CrisisTokens}(T).
  \label{eq:crisis_flag}
\end{equation}
Note that $(\hat{y} = c_3)$ in \eqref{eq:crisis_flag} means a severe
screening category triggers the crisis flag.
This distinguishes high symptom burden (severe PHQ-9 tier) from explicit
ideation indicators ($q_9 > 0$ or crisis tokens).
When $q_9 > 0$ or crisis tokens are detected, the screening category is
escalated regardless of the fused score:
\begin{equation}
  (q_9 > 0) \;\lor\; \text{CrisisTokens}(T)
  \;\Rightarrow\;
  \hat{y} \leftarrow c_3,\quad c \leftarrow \max(0.90,\; c).
  \label{eq:hre_escalate}
\end{equation}

% ── Major Comment 6B: Priority floor applied to all HRE conditions ────────
The rule-based priority floor $c \leftarrow \max(0.90,\; c)$ is applied
under all three HRE trigger conditions ($S \ge 20$, $q_9 > 0$, and crisis
token detection), not only the high-score condition.
This value denotes a minimum priority indicator for a rule-triggered outcome
and should not be interpreted as a calibrated posterior probability.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:fusion} details the complete decision flow.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Tri-Modal Fusion With Rule-Based High-Risk Escalation}
\label{alg:fusion}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE PHQ-9 answers $\mathbf{q}$, text $T$, audio $\mathbf{a}$
\ENSURE Screening category $\hat{y} \in \mathcal{C}$,
        priority score $c \in [0,1]$, crisis flag $C_F$
\STATE Compute $S \leftarrow \sum_{i=1}^9 q_i$
  \hfill $\triangleright$ \eqref{eq:phq_score}
\STATE $\mathbf{p}^Q \leftarrow \Phi_Q(S)$
  \hfill $\triangleright$ \eqref{eq:phq_probs}
\STATE $\mathbf{p}^T \leftarrow \text{NLP\_Branch}(T)$
  \hfill $\triangleright$ \eqref{eq:nlp_argmax}--\eqref{eq:nlp_normalize}
\STATE $\mathbf{p}^A \leftarrow \text{Acoustic\_Branch}(\mathbf{a})$
  \hfill $\triangleright$ \eqref{eq:acoustic_proxy}
\STATE $\mathbf{u} \leftarrow
  0.50\,\mathbf{p}^T + 0.30\,\mathbf{p}^A + 0.20\,\mathbf{p}^Q$
  \hfill $\triangleright$ \eqref{eq:fusion_weighted}
\STATE $\mathbf{p}^F \leftarrow \mathbf{u}/\!\sum_j u_j$
  \hfill $\triangleright$ \eqref{eq:fusion_normalize} (defensive)
\STATE $\hat{y} \leftarrow \arg\max_k p_k^F$;\quad
       $c     \leftarrow \max_k p_k^F$
  \hfill $\triangleright$ \eqref{eq:prediction}
\STATE $C_F \leftarrow \text{FALSE}$
\IF{$\hat{y} = c_3$}
  \STATE $C_F \leftarrow \text{TRUE}$; display resource routing
\ENDIF
\IF{$S \ge 20 \;\lor\; q_9 > 0 \;\lor\; \text{CrisisTokens}(T)$}
  \STATE $\hat{y} \leftarrow c_3$;\quad
         $c \leftarrow \max(0.90, c)$
    \hfill $\triangleright$ rule-based priority floor, \eqref{eq:hre_severe}--\eqref{eq:hre_escalate}
  \STATE $C_F \leftarrow \text{TRUE}$
  \STATE Display crisis-resource routing (Tele-MANAS, KIRAN numbers)
\ENDIF
\RETURN $\hat{y},\; c,\; \mathbf{p}^F,\; C_F$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Saathi: LLM-Assisted Conversational Companion}
% ── Minor Comment 4: "autonomous" → "LLM-assisted" ───────────────────────
MindScreen integrates \textbf{Saathi}, an LLM-assisted conversational
companion for ongoing emotional reflection.
Saathi operates through a hierarchical four-tier pipeline.
No clinical effectiveness evaluation of Saathi's responses has been performed;
it is presented as an implemented system component:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Crisis Resource Router:}
    Detects predefined crisis-related keywords in dialogue and immediately
    displays de-escalation text alongside national helpline information for
    Tele-MANAS ($14416$ / $1800$-$891$-$4416$) and KIRAN
    ($1800$-$599$-$0019$)~\cite{telemanas2022}.
    No automated emergency dispatch or integration with external emergency
    services is performed.
  \item \textbf{Conversational Neural Core:}
    Employs instruction-tuned large language models (Google Gemini~1.5 Flash
    via REST API or Mistral-7B-Instruct via HuggingFace) parameterized with 8
    turns of conversational history.
    Response factuality, hallucination, and safety under adversarial input
    have not been formally evaluated.
  \item \textbf{Affective Topic Classifier:}
    Routes user statements into six stress domains: family friction, academic
    pressure, insomnia, panic/anxiety, social isolation, and positive
    equilibrium.
  \item \textbf{Non-Pharmacological Intervention Routing:}
    Suggests structured somatic exercises (e.g., 4-7-8 Pranayama breathwork,
    5-4-3-2-1 sensory grounding) paced every 3--4 dialogue turns.
    These are supportive suggestions, not clinical treatments.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Evaluation Scope}
MindScreen is a full-stack screening support prototype.
System evaluation is conducted at two levels:
(i)~\textbf{functional correctness} of the deterministic HRE module under
structured masking scenarios, and
(ii)~\textbf{operational performance} of the deployment pipeline measured by
latency and memory profiling.

The evaluation does not report accuracy, macro-F1, precision, recall,
sensitivity, specificity, AUROC, or calibration metrics for the fused
classifier.
No labeled test-set evaluation of depression screening performance has been
conducted.
Large-scale prospective evaluation against an annotated clinical corpus
requires IRB-approved data collection and is reserved for future work
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{HRE Rule Execution Verification}
\label{sec:hre_verify}

The HRE module is designed to ensure that explicitly defined high-risk
indicators are not suppressed by modality averaging.
To verify that the implemented rule logic executes as specified, we evaluated
three manually constructed masking scenarios (Table~\ref{tab:safety}).
Each scenario is designed so that linear fusion without the HRE module
produces an incorrect output, while the HRE module produces the intended
output.

These scenarios establish that the rules execute correctly for the three
tested inputs.
They do not establish:
\begin{itemize}
  \item sensitivity to all forms of suicidal ideation;
  \item correct behavior under negation, ambiguity, code-switching, or
    indirect expressions;
  \item clinical utility in real patient populations; or
  \item sufficiency of the selected indicators for crisis triage.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ethical Considerations and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Scope and Non-Diagnostic Disclaimer}
MindScreen is an \textbf{adjunctive screening support prototype} and does not
perform clinical diagnosis, replace comprehensive psychiatric evaluation under
DSM-5 or ICD-11 criteria, or constitute a validated clinical screening or
triage tool.
Outputs are risk-indicator signals intended to prompt timely consultation with
qualified clinicians.

\subsection{Privacy-Oriented Implementation Mechanisms}
The following mechanisms have been implemented; they do not constitute
compliance with healthcare privacy regulations, which would require formal
regulatory assessment:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Transient Audio Processing:}
    Voice recordings are processed in server memory as volatile byte streams;
    no raw audio is stored on disk or in persistent databases.
  \item \textbf{Cryptographic Mechanisms:}
    User records use bcrypt hashing (12 rounds) and HS256-signed JWTs.
    JWT expiration, token refresh, and secret-key management are handled at
    the application level; formal security audit has not been conducted.
  \item \textbf{Keyword Feature Attribution:}
    Influential lexical tokens are surfaced to support interpretability;
    text entries may be temporarily accessible to external NLP API providers.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Acknowledged Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{No Predictive Clinical Evaluation:}
    The framework has not been evaluated against a labeled clinical dataset.
    No accuracy, F1, AUROC, or calibration metrics have been reported.
    Prospective clinical validation trials under IRB oversight are required
    before any healthcare deployment.
  \item \textbf{Heuristic Score Distributions:}
    All modality outputs ($\mathbf{p}^Q$, $\mathbf{p}^T$, $\mathbf{p}^A$,
    $\mathbf{p}^F$) are normalized heuristic score vectors, not calibrated
    probability distributions.
    The correspondence between predicted values and empirical class frequencies
    has not been evaluated.
  \item \textbf{Acoustic Payload Heuristic:}
    Production acoustic analysis uses decoded audio payload size as a
    heuristic proxy.
    This is an engineering simplification; it does not extract $F_0$, MFCCs,
    or prosodic features, and has not been demonstrated to provide clinically
    meaningful acoustic information.
  \item \textbf{Heuristic Affective Proxy Mapping:}
    The emotion-to-severity mapping is a design approximation; direct
    fine-tuning on labeled clinical corpora (e.g., DAIC-WOZ, CLPsych) would
    be required for clinical meaningfulness.
  \item \textbf{Manually Specified Fusion Weights:}
    The weights $w_T = 0.50$, $w_A = 0.30$, $w_Q = 0.20$ reflect design
    assumptions; empirical optimization via cross-validation on labeled
    clinical data has not been performed.
  \item \textbf{HRE Rule Limitations:}
    The crisis token set uses simple string matching without negation handling,
    context awareness, or false-positive evaluation.
    The three verification scenarios establish rule execution, not clinical
    sensitivity or safety.
  \item \textbf{Saathi Safety Evaluation:}
    Saathi's LLM responses have not been evaluated for factuality, harmful
    content, hallucination, or unsafe behavior under adversarial or
    ambiguous inputs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented \textbf{MindScreen}, a tri-modal prototype for depression
screening support combining PHQ-9 psychometric scoring
\eqref{eq:phq_score}--\eqref{eq:phq_probs}, a text-based heuristic affective
classifier \eqref{eq:nlp_argmax}--\eqref{eq:nlp_normalize}, and an
audio-payload-based production heuristic \eqref{eq:acoustic_proxy} through
decision-level late fusion.
% ── Moderate Comment 4: "more informationally rich" → architecture claim ──
The architecture combines information from questionnaire, text, and
audio-related inputs into a single decision pipeline.
The rule-based HRE module \eqref{eq:hre_severe}--\eqref{eq:hre_escalate}
prevents predefined high-risk indicators from being suppressed by modality
averaging; rule execution was verified under three manually constructed
masking scenarios (Table~\ref{tab:safety}).
The system does not establish clinical screening accuracy, calibration, or
clinical safety.
Saathi provides an LLM-assisted conversational companion with crisis-resource
routing and display of national helpline information (Tele-MANAS, KIRAN).

Future work will address the following priorities:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Labeled clinical evaluation:}
    IRB-approved evaluation of the full fusion pipeline against a labeled
    clinical corpus (e.g., DAIC-WOZ, CLPsych), reporting accuracy,
    macro-F1, AUROC, and calibration metrics for each modality and fusion
    configuration.
  \item \textbf{Calibration:}
    Assessment of output calibration via expected calibration error (ECE)
    on a held-out set, followed by a calibration procedure if required.
  \item \textbf{Acoustic feature extraction:}
    Replacement of the payload-size heuristic with client-side WebAudio
    WebAssembly (Wasm) on-device MFCC and $F_0$ extraction within cloud
    memory constraints.
  \item \textbf{Fusion weight optimization:}
    Empirical optimization of $w_T$, $w_A$, $w_Q$ via cross-validation on
    labeled data, with a weight-sensitivity analysis comparing equal, modality-
    dominant, and proposed weighting configurations.
  \item \textbf{HRE robustness evaluation:}
    Systematic evaluation of the keyword-matching component on positive,
    negative, ambiguous, and indirect examples, including negation and
    third-party reference cases.
  \item \textbf{Saathi safety assessment:}
    Structured test suite covering crisis and non-crisis prompts, expected
    routing behavior, LLM fallback conditions, and human-review procedures.
\end{enumerate}
