\begin{abstract}
This paper presents \textbf{MindScreen}, a deployed, full-stack mental-health
screening support system that fuses three complementary signal sources---
Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) responses, transformer-based text
emotion classification, and client-side Web Audio acoustic feature extraction
(RMS loudness, vocal variability, spectral centroid, and speaking ratio)---through
decision-level late fusion.
A key architectural contribution is the \textbf{High-Risk Escalation (HRE)}
module: a formally specified deterministic layer that prevents high-risk
indicators (PHQ-9 Item~9 endorsement, score $S \ge 20$, or verified affirmative
crisis intent) from being diluted by modality averaging.
The text branch integrates a windowed negation and scope-resolution filter
to suppress false-positive triggers on negated phrases and third-party attributions.
The system is deployed on a cloud-constrained container (0.5~vCPU, 512~MB
RAM) and integrates \textbf{Saathi}, an LLM-assisted conversational companion
backed by Google Gemini~2.5 Flash with crisis-resource routing.
Evaluation comprises: (i)~HRE anti-dilution verification and 5-way weight
sensitivity ablation across five clinical archetypes; (ii)~negation-aware
disambiguation reducing false positives by 88.9\%; and (iii)~latency profiling
showing 13.2~ms local end-to-end and 512~ms cloud-augmented latency.
The system is explicitly framed as an engineering screening support prototype.
No clinical validation or diagnostic accuracy has been established; both are
identified as primary future work.
\end{abstract}

Multimodal late fusion, depression screening, PHQ-9, transformer NLP,

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) affects over 280 million people globally and
is the leading cause of non-fatal disability~\cite{who2023}.
In India, the mental healthcare treatment gap exceeds 75\%--85\% due to a
shortage of practitioners ($\approx$0.75 per 100{,}000 population) and
entrenched social stigma~\cite{gururaj2016}.
The PHQ-9~\cite{kroenke2001} is a widely used, validated screening
instrument, yet self-reports alone cannot capture real-time affective language
or vocal cues that may indicate distress.

Automated multimodal screening tools address this gap, but unconstrained
probabilistic averaging introduces a critical failure mode:
\textit{false-negative dilution}.
If a user reports acute suicidal ideation on the PHQ-9 Item~9 or writes crisis
language in free text, but exhibits calm vocal acoustics, linear weighted
averaging can push the composite risk below the high-risk threshold---missing
a flagged individual entirely.
No existing deployed system we are aware of addresses this failure mode with
a formally specified deterministic override within a lightweight,
cloud-deployed architecture.

MindScreen addresses this by combining:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{tri-modal late-fusion pipeline} over PHQ-9, text, and
    audio signal sources;
  \item A \textbf{High-Risk Escalation (HRE) module}---a deterministic
    rule layer that bypasses fusion when explicit high-risk indicators are
    present, with formal mathematical specification and verification;
  \item A \textbf{conversational companion (Saathi)} with LLM-backed
    empathetic dialogue and crisis-resource routing to national helplines; and
  \item A \textbf{cloud-native deployment} verified to run within 0.5~vCPU
    / 512~MB RAM with end-to-end latency under 25~ms for local inference.
\end{enumerate}

The system is presented as a screening support prototype.
Its outputs are risk indicators to inform timely clinician consultation, not
diagnostic labels.
No clinical validation has been conducted; this is explicitly acknowledged
throughout.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Existing mental-health screening systems fall into three paradigms.
\textit{Text-only systems}~\cite{ji2022,xu2024} achieve strong benchmark
metrics but ignore somatic signals.
\textit{Unimodal acoustic systems}~\cite{cummins2015,scherer2013} require
controlled recording environments unsuitable for consumer devices.
\textit{Multimodal systems}~\cite{ringeval2019,devault2014} predominantly use
early-feature concatenation, which is sensitive to modality missingness and
produces opaque intermediate representations.
Conversational screening tools such as Woebot~\cite{fitzpatrick2017} are
clinically validated via RCT but operate on text alone and apply only static
safety prompts.

Table~\ref{tab:related} positions MindScreen against these approaches.
Two distinguishing design properties are: (a)~decision-level late fusion that
preserves per-modality interpretability, and (b)~a formally specified HRE
module that provides deterministic high-risk override behavior absent from the
compared systems.

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Input Space and Output Taxonomy}

An assessment session accepts a tuple
$\mathcal{X} = (\mathbf{q},\, T,\, \mathbf{a})$ where
$\mathbf{q} \in \{0,1,2,3\}^9$ are the PHQ-9 item responses,
$T \in \mathcal{V}^*$ is a free-text journal entry, and
$\mathbf{a}$ is the captured audio signal.
The system outputs a screening category $\hat{y}$ drawn from the
4-tier set:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C} = \{c_0\!:\!\text{minimal},\;c_1\!:\!\text{mild},\;
                  c_2\!:\!\text{moderate},\;c_3\!:\!\text{severe}\},
  \label{eq:label_space}
\end{equation}
together with a priority score $c \in [0,1]$ and a Boolean crisis flag
$C_F$.

MindScreen maps PHQ-9 scores to this 4-tier space by merging the clinically
adjacent moderately-severe (15--19) and severe (20--27) PHQ-9 bands into a
single high-priority tier (Table~\ref{tab:phq_map}).
Both bands trigger professional-referral actions in the implemented system,
justifying the merger.
Users should not equate the system's ``severe'' label with the clinical
PHQ-9 threshold of $S \ge 20$.

\label{tab:phq_map}
\centering

\textbf{Score $S$} & \textbf{PHQ-9 Band} & \textbf{MindScreen Category}
  & \textbf{Action} \\
\midrule

\subsection{Modality Processing Pipelines}

\subsubsection{PHQ-9 Branch ($\mathbf{p}^Q$)}
The aggregate score $S = \sum_{i=1}^{9} q_i$ is mapped to a fixed 4-class
score vector via a piecewise function $\Phi_Q$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}^Q =
  \begin{cases}
    [0.80,\;0.15,\;0.05,\;0.00]^T, & 0  \le S \le 4  \\
    [0.10,\;0.70,\;0.15,\;0.05]^T, & 5  \le S \le 9  \\
    [0.00,\;0.15,\;0.70,\;0.15]^T, & 10 \le S \le 14 \\
    [0.00,\;0.05,\;0.15,\;0.80]^T, & 15 \le S \le 27
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:phq_probs}
\end{equation}
These vectors are design constants grounded in PHQ-9 clinical cutoffs; they
are not estimated from labeled data.

\subsubsection{NLP Text Branch ($\mathbf{p}^T$)}
Free-text input $T$ is classified by \textit{j-hartmann/emotion-english-distilroberta-base}~\cite{hartmann2022},
accessed via the HuggingFace Inference API.
The top emotion class $e^*$ and its softmax confidence $s$ are extracted:
\begin{equation}
  (e^*,\; s) = \arg\max_{e \in \mathcal{E}}\;
    P(e \mid T;\,\Theta_{\text{NLP}}).
  \label{eq:nlp_argmax}
\end{equation}
A heuristic mapping $\Gamma: \mathcal{E} \to \{0,1,2,3\}$ translates the
7-class emotion output to a screening index:
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma(e^*) =
  \begin{cases}
    0, & e^* \in \{\text{joy},\;\text{neutral},\;\text{surprise}\} \\
    1, & e^* \in \{\text{fear},\;\text{anger}\} \\
    2, & e^* \in \{\text{sadness},\;\text{disgust}\}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:emotion_map}
\end{equation}
If affirmative crisis intent is detected in $T$ via windowed negation
and scope resolution (evaluating triggers such as ``suicide'', ``kill myself'',
``hopeless'', while suppressing negated expressions such as ``do not want to die''
and third-party media references), the index is escalated: $\Gamma(e^*) \leftarrow 3$.
The score vector $\mathbf{p}^T$ is then formed by assigning $s$ to the
predicted class and $0.10$ to each remaining class, followed by
$\ell_1$ normalization.
This emotion-to-risk mapping is a design heuristic; its relationship to
clinical depression severity has not been empirically validated.
When the HuggingFace API is unreachable, a deterministic keyword-rule fallback
maintains service availability.

\subsubsection{Audio Branch ($\mathbf{p}^A$)}
To bypass the memory constraints of hosting heavy acoustic libraries
(such as PyTorch and Librosa exceeding 512~MB RAM) on cloud containers,
MindScreen offloads signal extraction to the client browser via the standard
Web Audio API.
During voice input, an \texttt{AnalyserNode} computes six real-time acoustic
descriptors over the time and frequency domains: mean RMS loudness
($\text{RMS}_\mu$), RMS variability ($\text{RMS}_\sigma$), zero-crossing rate
($\text{ZCR}$), normalized spectral centroid ($S_C$), 85\% spectral rolloff
($S_R$), and speaking-to-silence ratio ($R_{\text{speak}}$).
These features capture vocal energy flattening, monotonicity, and speech
hesitancy associated with depressive affect~\cite{cummins2015}.

The normalized feature vector $\mathbf{f} \in [0,1]^6$ is transmitted to the
server, where a linear composite depression index $I_D \in [0,1]$ is computed:
\begin{equation}
  I_D = \sum_{j=1}^5 \alpha_j \, \phi_j(\mathbf{f}),
  \label{eq:acoustic_index}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_j$ measures deviation toward depressive characteristics
(reduced energy $1 - \text{RMS}_\mu$, reduced variability
$1 - \min(\text{RMS}_\sigma/0.25, 1)$, low spectral centroid $1 - S_C$,
hesitancy $1 - R_{\text{speak}}$, and abnormal ZCR), weighted by design
constants $\boldsymbol{\alpha} = [0.25, 0.20, 0.20, 0.25, 0.10]^T$.
The index $I_D$ is then mapped to the 4-class score vector $\mathbf{p}^A$
via piecewise linear interpolation across calibrated thresholds.
If voice input is omitted by the user, a neutral prior vector
$[0.25, 0.45, 0.20, 0.10]^T$ is assigned.

\subsection{Decision-Level Late Fusion and Calibration}
\label{sec:fusion}

The three score vectors are combined as a convex sum:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}^F = w_T\,\mathbf{p}^T + w_A\,\mathbf{p}^A + w_Q\,\mathbf{p}^Q,
  \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
with weights $w_T = 0.50$, $w_A = 0.30$, $w_Q = 0.20$ ($\sum w_m = 1$).
Because each $\mathbf{p}^m$ sums to one and the weights sum to one,
$\mathbf{p}^F$ already lies on the simplex; normalization is retained
defensively.
Following convex combination, the distribution is calibrated using
temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017} ($T = 1.20$) to soften heuristic overconfidence:
\begin{equation}
  p_k^{\text{cal}} = \frac{\exp(\log(p_k^F)/T)}{\sum_{j=1}^4 \exp(\log(p_j^F)/T)}.
  \label{eq:temperature}
\end{equation}
The base screening category and calibrated confidence are:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = \arg\max_k p_k^{\text{cal}},
  \qquad
  c = \max_k p_k^{\text{cal}}.
  \label{eq:prediction}
\end{equation}

\subsection{High-Risk Escalation (HRE) Module}
\label{sec:hre}

Decision-level fusion still exposes the false-negative dilution problem: a
user who scores $S = 22$ on the PHQ-9 but generates joy-emotion text and
active audio will produce a fused vector dominated by the text and audio
channels, potentially outputting ``mild.''
The HRE module resolves this by applying two deterministic rules
\textit{after} fusion, unconditionally overriding the fused output when
explicit high-risk indicators are present.

\noindent\textbf{Rule~1 — High-score override.} If $S \ge 20$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} \leftarrow c_3,\quad
  c \leftarrow \max(0.90,\; c).
  \label{eq:hre_severe}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Rule~2 — Explicit-indicator escalation.}
If $q_9 > 0$ (suicidal ideation endorsed on PHQ-9 Item~9) or a crisis token
is detected in $T$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} \leftarrow c_3,\quad
  c \leftarrow \max(0.90,\; c),\quad
  C_F \leftarrow \text{TRUE}.
  \label{eq:hre_escalate}
\end{equation}
$C_F$ is also set TRUE when $\hat{y} = c_3$ after fusion, even without an
explicit indicator.
In all escalation cases, the system immediately displays crisis-resource
information (Tele-MANAS helpline~\cite{telemanas2022}, KIRAN).

The value $c \leftarrow \max(0.90, c)$ is a rule-triggered priority
indicator, not a calibrated posterior probability.
Table~\ref{tab:hre} enumerates the complete HRE decision matrix.

\begin{table}[!t]
\caption{HRE Module: Complete Decision Matrix}
\label{tab:hre}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.8cm}cccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Trigger} & $\hat{y}$ & $C_F$ & \textbf{Helplines} & $c$ \\
\midrule
No trigger (fused output) & \eqref{eq:prediction} & F & No & \eqref{eq:prediction} \\
$S \ge 20$                & $c_3$ & T & Yes & $\max(0.90,c)$ \\
$q_9 > 0$                 & $c_3$ & T & Yes & $\max(0.90,c)$ \\
Crisis token in $T$       & $c_3$ & T & Yes & $\max(0.90,c)$ \\
Fused $\hat{y} = c_3$     & $c_3$ & T & Yes & unchanged \\
Multiple triggers         & $c_3$ & T & Yes & $\max(0.90,c)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:fusion} gives the complete inference flow.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{MindScreen Tri-Modal Inference with HRE}
\label{alg:fusion}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $\mathbf{q}$, $T$, $\mathbf{a}$
\ENSURE $\hat{y}$, $c$, $C_F$
\STATE $\mathbf{p}^Q \leftarrow \Phi_Q\!\left(\sum q_i\right)$
  \hfill $\triangleright$ \eqref{eq:phq_probs}
\STATE $\mathbf{p}^T \leftarrow \text{NLP}(T)$;
  $\;\mathbf{p}^A \leftarrow \text{Audio}(\mathbf{a})$
\STATE $\mathbf{p}^F \leftarrow
  0.50\,\mathbf{p}^T + 0.30\,\mathbf{p}^A + 0.20\,\mathbf{p}^Q$
  \hfill $\triangleright$ \eqref{eq:fusion}
\STATE $\hat{y} \leftarrow \arg\max_k p_k^F$;\;
  $c \leftarrow \max_k p_k^F$;\;
  $C_F \leftarrow \text{FALSE}$
\IF{$\hat{y} = c_3$}
  \STATE $C_F \leftarrow \text{TRUE}$; display helplines
\ENDIF
\IF{$S \ge 20 \lor q_9 > 0 \lor \text{CrisisTokens}(T)$}
  \STATE $\hat{y} \leftarrow c_3$;\;
    $c \leftarrow \max(0.90,c)$;\;
    $C_F \leftarrow \text{TRUE}$
  \hfill $\triangleright$ \eqref{eq:hre_severe}--\eqref{eq:hre_escalate}
  \STATE Display crisis-resource helplines
\ENDIF
\RETURN $\hat{y},\; c,\; C_F$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Saathi: LLM-Assisted Conversational Companion}
\label{sec:saathi}

Saathi is an LLM-assisted conversational component backed by Google Gemini~2.5
Flash (primary) or Mistral-7B-Instruct (fallback), maintaining 8-turn
conversational context.
It operates in four sequential stages:
(1)~a \textit{crisis safety gate} utilizing linguistic negation and
scope-resolved intent analysis to immediately surface national helpline
information if affirmative crisis signals appear;
(2)~\textit{affective topic routing} classifying user statements into six
domains (family, academics, insomnia, anxiety, isolation, positive);
(3)~\textit{LLM response generation} conditioned on the classified domain and
conversation history; and
(4)~\textit{structured intervention suggestions} (4-7-8 breathing, 5-4-3-2-1
grounding) interleaved every 3--4 turns.
Saathi does not provide clinical advice; it directs users to professional
resources.
LLM response safety and factuality have not been formally evaluated.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{HRE Rule Execution Verification and Weight Sensitivity}

We evaluated HRE execution across three adversarial masking scenarios
(Table~\ref{tab:hre_verify}) where modality signals conflict, causing linear
fusion alone to produce false negatives.
In TC-1 ($S=22 \ge 20$), joy text and active audio push linear fusion to
``mild''; Rule~1 \eqref{eq:hre_severe} deterministically forces ``severe''.
In TC-2 ($S=3, q_9=1$), linear fusion produces ``minimal''; Rule~2
\eqref{eq:hre_escalate} escalates the output and activates $C_F$.
In TC-3, affirmative crisis text overrides low questionnaire scores.

To analyze the impact of design constants, we executed an ablation across five
weighting schemes: Equal (33/33/33), Text-Dominant (60/20/20), Audio-Dominant
(20/60/20), Questionnaire-Dominant (20/20/60), and MindScreen (50/30/20).
Under concordant severe and minimal profiles, all five schemes converged
consistently (severe: $0.56$--$0.75$, minimal: $0.65$--$0.80$).
Under masked affect ($S=20$, positive text), all linear schemes failed
(predicting minimal, $0.37$--$0.60$ confidence), and were rescued only by the
HRE override.
Under implicit ideation ($S=2, q_9=1$), questionnaire-dominant weighting
dangerously predicted minimal ($0.53$), which the HRE module rescued to severe
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
This demonstrates that unconstrained averaging is structurally vulnerable
regardless of the chosen linear weights, establishing the necessity of the HRE
deterministic bound.

\subsection{Negation-Aware Linguistic Intent Disambiguation}

To resolve the false-positive vulnerability of naive keyword matching, we
evaluated our windowed negation and scope-resolution service over an adversarial
corpus of 14 distress and negated test statements (Table~\ref{tab:negation}).
Under naive substring matching, negated phrases (e.g., ``I do not want to die'',
``would never commit suicide'') and third-party references (e.g., ``my friend
talked about suicide'') caused an 88.9\% false-positive rate (8/9 false alarms).
The implemented scope-resolution filter achieved a 0.0\% false-positive rate
(0/9 false alarms, an 88.9\% absolute reduction) while retaining 100\%
sensitivity on true affirmative crisis assertions.

\subsection{Deployment Latency and Memory}
\label{sec:latency}

All measurements were taken on the production Render.com free-tier container
(0.5~vCPU, 512~MB RAM, Ubuntu 22.04, Python 3.11, FastAPI 0.111) under
sequential single-request load.
Each local component was timed over 50 invocations after a 10-call warm-up;
P95 is from raw per-invocation observations.
Hosted-API latencies include round-trip network time (US-East region).
Memory values are peak Python-managed heap allocations measured via
\texttt{tracemalloc}; they do not capture total process RSS.

pipeline.
These figures characterize prototype performance under the stated single-user
benchmark; concurrent-load behavior and sustained operation have not been
tested.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{No clinical validation.}
MindScreen has not been evaluated against a labeled clinical dataset.
No predictive accuracy, F-score, AUROC, or calibration metrics are reported.
The system is an adjunctive screening support tool; outputs are not diagnostic
labels.
IRB-approved trials on a labeled clinical cohort are the primary next step,
targeting full per-modality and fused classification metrics.

\textbf{Heuristic signal sources.}
All three score vectors ($\mathbf{p}^Q$, $\mathbf{p}^T$, $\mathbf{p}^A$) are
constructed from design constants rather than learned parameters.
While the audio branch extracts real-time acoustic descriptors
(RMS energy, variability, and spectral centroid) via the client Web Audio API,
it maps them using a heuristic linear composite index rather than a deep
acoustic model trained on clinical recordings.
Future work will evaluate on-device ONNX/WebAssembly neural feature extractors
to maintain memory constraints while capturing complex temporal vocal patterns.

\textbf{Linguistic scope boundaries.}
While windowed negation and third-party scope resolution have been integrated,
detecting subtle metaphorical, sarcastic, or culturally implicit distress
remains a limitation.
Future work will evaluate lightweight zero-shot NLI models for deeper semantic
contextualization.

\textbf{Fusion weight selection.}
The weights $w_T=0.50$, $w_A=0.30$, $w_Q=0.20$ are design choices.
Cross-validation on labeled data and an ablation study across weighting
configurations are planned once ground-truth labels are available.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented MindScreen, a full-stack multimodal mental-health
screening support system built around three principles: decision-level late
fusion for modality independence, a formally specified deterministic HRE
module that prevents high-risk indicators from being diluted by averaging, and
a cloud-native deployment that fits within a free-tier 512~MB container.
The HRE module was verified to produce the correct output across three
adversarial masking scenarios where fusion alone fails.
Latency profiling confirmed 13.2~ms local and 512~ms cloud-augmented
end-to-end performance.
The system is honest about what it does not establish: no clinical accuracy
metrics are claimed, and IRB-approved clinical evaluation is identified as the
required next step for real-world deployment.
